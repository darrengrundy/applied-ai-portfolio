| In any period of | Maximum work | Minimum rest |
|---|---|---|
| 5.5 h | 5.25 h | 15 continuous minutes |
| 8 h | 7.5 h | 30 min in blocks of 15 continuous minutes |
| 11 h | 10 h | 60 min in blocks of 15 continuous minutes |
| 24 h | 12 h | 7 continuous hours stationary rest |
| 7 days | 72 h | 24 continuous hours stationary rest |
| 14 days | 144 h | 2 night rest breaks, and 2 night rest breaks on consecutive days |

Night rest break: 7 continuous hours stationary rest between 22:00 and 08:00 next day (driver's base time zone), or 24 continuous hours stationary rest.
Stationary rest: out of the vehicle, or in an approved sleeper berth of a stationary vehicle.

## F3. Fatigue duty (same page)
From 1 August 2026 a driver must not drive any heavy vehicle over 4.5 t GVM while impaired by fatigue or unfit to drive, even within the hours. CoR parties must, so far as reasonably practicable, ensure their activities do not cause or encourage a driver to exceed the limits. BFM and AFM are transitional (existing accreditations continue until expiry); ACH under ACA–Fatigue replaces them for new applicants.

## F4. Counting time
URL: https://www.nhvr.gov.au/safety-accreditation-compliance/fatigue-management/counting-time (transitional banner, Aug 2026)
- Periods under 24 h are counted forward from the end of any rest break.
- Standard hours and ACH: all periods of 24 h or longer (7, 14, 28 days) are counted forward from the end of the longest major rest break required for that period (7-day from a 24 h break; 14-day from a night rest break). If the required break has not been taken, count from the end of any rest break.
- A later major rest within a 24 h counting period does not reset it; work before and after counts. Restarting early creates an overlapping 24 h period.
- EWD: counted in one-minute periods; a continuous rest under 15 minutes does not count toward a minimum rest requirement. (Written diary: work rounds up, rest rounds down, to 15 minutes.)
- Time is counted in the time zone of the driver's base.

## M1. General Mass Limits
URL: https://www.nhvr.gov.au/road-access/mass-and-dimension/mass-limits/general-mass-limits (post-Aug 2026 amendments)
- Steer axle, other motor vehicle: 6.0 t; complying steer axle vehicle: 6.5 t.
- Tandem axle group, dual tyres (not pig trailer): 17.0 t.
- Tri-axle group, dual tyres (not pig trailer): 21.0 t.
- Combination limit (Table 1): complying steer axle vehicle, not B-double/road train: 44.0 t; other vehicle not B-double/road train: 43.5 t.
- Mass is the lowest of the Table 1 limit, the sum of axle group limits, and the reduced-mass-amount limit; never above manufacturer ratings (GVM/GCM/ATM).

## L1. Load Restraint Guide 2025 (NHVR, 4th edition; 2018 edition remains valid)
Case study 1 extract: https://wws.nhvr.gov.au/document/691 (pages 205–207)
- Performance standards: restraint must withstand 0.8 g forward, 0.5 g rearward, 0.5 g sideways, and 0.2 g vertical relative to the vehicle (Loading performance standards, MDL Regulation Schedule 7; guide p. 4).
- A load is blocked only if within 200 mm of a rated headboard and cumulative front-to-rear gaps are under 200 mm (p. 206).
- Unrated equipment should not be used for restraint (p. 205).
